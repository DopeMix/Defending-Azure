**MICROSOFT SENTINEL ARCHITECTURE**

- MS sentinel core component is Log Analytics Workspaces (LAWs)
- LAW is an Azure **_resource_** where the logs are stored.

Implementing MS Sentinel (options)
<br></br>
- **_Single Tenant_** - Single Log Analytics workspace
  - **_Central repo_** for all logs all resources
    - Central pane of glass
    - Consolidated logs
  - **_Potential concerns_** - Logs will travel across Azure regions resulting to:
    - **_Bandwidth costs_** due to logs travelling across regions
    - **_Data governance issues_** due to data residency requirements
  - This is the "**_Standard Hub_**" approach. It is best for organizations that operate primarily in one
    region or have no strict legal requirement to keep data siloed.
    - **Usage Example:** A mid-sized retail company based in the US with some smaller branch offices in Europe.
    - **Why choose this:** They want a single SOC (Security Operations Center) team to manage all alerts from one dashboard.
      By keeping everything in one LAW, they can easily correlate a "suspicious login" in New York with "data exfiltration"
      from a server in London.
    - **The Cost Factor:** Even though they have servers in London, they choose to pay the egress fees (bandwidth costs) to move
      those logs to the US workspace because the simplicity of having one set of detection rules outweighs the extra $0.02–$0.05
      per GB for data transfer.
      <br></br>
- **_Single Tenant_** - Multiple Log Analytics workspaces (Regional)
  - In order to address the logs travelling across regions, regional LAWs can be created
    in different regions.
  - Although this might address bandwidth costs and data governance concerns, on the other hand,
    it will bring other concerns, such as:
    - No central pane of glass
    - Granular access control
    - Granular retention settings
- This is the "**Compliance-First**" approach. It is used when data residency is a legal "must-have" or when the volume of data is so massive
  that regional bandwidth costs become a major line item.
  - **Usage Example:** A global Swiss bank. Due to banking secrecy and GDPR laws, data from their German branch cannot leave Germany,
    and data from their Singapore branch must stay in Singapore.
  - **Why choose this:** They create one LAW in Germany West Central and another in Southeast Asia. This ensures they meet Data Residency laws.
  - **The Trade-off:** The SOC team now has a "fragmented" view. To see everything, they must use Cross-Workspace Queries. For example, a KQL query would look like:
    > union workspace("Germany-LAW").SecurityEvent, workspace("Singapore-LAW").SecurityEvent
      | where EventID == 4624
  - **Management Tip:** You can use Azure Monitor Workbooks to create a single dashboard that pulls data from all these regional workspaces simultaneously.
  <br></br>
- **_Multi-Tenant_**
  - If LAWs are not home tenants, then a multi-tenant architecture will be needed
  - An example of this architecture would be cloud service providers.
    - i.e., When a security organization needs to monitor other organizations
      Microsoft Sentinel deployments
  - This is the "Service Provider" approach. It is the gold standard for Managed Security Service Providers (MSSPs) or massive conglomerates with
    completely independent subsidiaries.
    - **Usage Example:** A cybersecurity firm (MSSP) managing 50 different small business clients.
    - **Why choose this:** Each client owns their own Azure Tenant and pays for their own Sentinel consumption. The MSSP uses Azure Lighthouse to
      "reach into" each client's tenant.
    - **The Benefit:** The MSSP analysts don't have to log out and log in 50 times a day. They stay in their own home tenant and see a consolidated
      "Incidents" view across all 50 clients.
    - **Intellectual Property:** The MSSP can keep their secret "hunting queries" and "playbooks" in their own tenant but run them against the customers' data.

**Summary of Considerations**
- **Tenancy:** Do you own the data, or are you managing it for someone else?
- **Compliance:** Are you in a regulated industry (Finance, Healthcare, Government)?
- **Region:** Is your data volume high enough that "Inter-region" transfer costs ($) start to hurt?
- **Access:** Does your "HR" team need to see HR logs, but "IT" shouldn't? Multiple LAWs make this easier to wall off.
